Balade

Français : README.fr.md

Cursors that stroll around any web page, like a team of colleagues working alongside you. Plain JavaScript, object-oriented, no bundler, no dependencies.

Install

chrome://extensions → turn on Developer mode → "Load unpacked" → pick this folder.

  • Alt+B starts or puts away the cursors on the current tab (change it in chrome://extensions/shortcuts).
  • Click the icon to open the menu: Start/Put away button, theme and settings. Settings are saved (chrome.storage.sync) and apply live on the page.
  • Esc puts the cursors away (during a game, Esc ends the game instead).

Sounds start after your first click or key press on the page (a browser rule). The extension can't run on Chrome's protected pages (chrome://, Chrome Web Store).

The extension's interface (menu, options, speech bubbles) is currently in French.

Try it without installing

demo.html loads the same scripts and shows the menu next to the page. For the menu to drive the page, serve the folder locally (for example python3 -m http.server) and open http://localhost:8000/demo.html. Opened directly as a file, the cursors work but the menu is decorative.

Themes

Theme Spirit
Ghost (default) cursor filled with its own color, white outline — the original v0.1 look
Classic pointers with the proportions of an operating system: white body, black outline
Riso zine two risograph inks, slightly off-register
Clay pebbles plump, matte shapes in clay colors
Field notebook inked lines, numbered specimens, red pencil

Each theme draws five pointers (arrow, hand, text, crosshair, busy), all in SVG. The pointer changes like in a real browser: a hand over links and buttons, an I-beam over fields, a crosshair over images, busy during some pauses.

How they move

  • Curved paths: chained circular arcs rather than straight lines.
  • Human timing: duration from Fitts's law, a minimum-jerk speed profile, an aim followed by a small correction, then a click that presses down.
  • Moods: each cursor has a temperament (calm, curious, busy…) and alternates strolling, fidgeting and inspecting the page.
  • Jostling: your mouse pushes the cursors away; a damped spring brings them back.
  • Typing: curious cursors type ghost text into empty fields (drawn on top, the field's value never changes).
  • Sounds: one voice per cursor, in a pentatonic scale, panned to where the cursor is.

Anticipation and habits

  • Guess my moves: from the direction and braking of your mouse, a cursor heads for the element you're aiming at, often before you get there.
  • Learn my habits: Balade remembers your click sequences on each site and waits for you on the next step when it is predictable enough.

Everything stays on this computer; only the identity of clicked elements is stored, never what you type. The menu shows what has been learned on the site and lets you forget it. Consider excluding sensitive sites ("This site" section).

Per-site settings

In the menu, the "This site" section lets you never run Balade on a site (useful for internal tools or pages showing sensitive data), or start it there automatically. Auto-start asks for access to that site, and that site only.

Dark pages

Each cursor looks at the background under it and adapts its pointer when it is dark ("Adapt to dark pages" switch).

Social life and reactions

  • Shared attention: keep your mouse still for about a second, or type in a field, and the curious ones come to look; they leave when you move away.
  • Encounters: a wave, a little sound, sometimes one follows the other.
  • Reactions: a rush toward your clicks, a cheer when you submit a form.
  • Swarm on demand: press Shift, then hold Shift.

Two menu switches control these: "Chat with each other" and "React to what I do".

Games and tools

From the menu:

  • Play tag: catch the cursors with your mouse (Esc to stop).
  • Focus · 25 min: the cursors leave, then come back for the break.
  • Record a video of the tab: a WebM file downloaded when you stop (2 minutes max).

Customization

"Customize…" link at the bottom of the menu (or right-click the icon → Options): your own names, a custom theme (starting style, colors, imported SVG pointer, sanitized), with a preview.

Scrolling

The cursors live on the page, not on the screen. When you scroll, they move with the content, then each one catches up after a reaction time (0.3 to 1.5 s depending on its patience; sometimes longer if it is "busy"). A cursor out of view shows up as a marker with its name at the top or bottom of the screen.

Project layout

Every file declares its classes on window.Balade. With no bundler, they are injected in the order of FILES (lib/inject.js), which is also the order of the <script> tags in demo.html.

Adding a behavior

  1. Create src/behaviors/MyBehavior.js: a class that extends Behavior and implements *run(cursor).
  2. Add it to FILES (lib/inject.js) and to the <script> tags in demo.html, before Engine.js.
  3. Pick it in Engine.chooseBehavior(cursor).

What is never touched

Clicks are simulated (the cursor presses down, a sound plays); no event is sent to the page. Ghost text is drawn on top of the field: the field's value never changes. Password, disabled, read-only, already filled or in-use fields are skipped.

Motion math: why these implementations

Identical results, cheaper computations (measured with node /tmp/opencode/bench-trig.mjs, 200k gestures × 24 steps):

  • Arc tracing (motion/Path.js): the turtle heading vector is rotated iteratively (complex multiply by a fixed per-arc rotation) instead of Math.cos/sin per step — ~4x faster (~90 vs ~380 ns/gesture), bit-exact (drift 1e-16). Rejected: a 256-entry sin table (3x slower than Math.cos — memory access costs more than the FPU) and Taylor order 5 (slower and wrong, error 0.5 near ±π).
  • Gesture timing (motion/Motion.js): the progress curve minjerk(t^q) is sampled once per gesture (64 entries, q is fixed per gesture) instead of Math.pow per frame — same curve within 5e-4, indexed read per frame.
  • Swarm steering (behaviors/Swarm.js): sin(3θ+φ) expanded by triple-angle from the already-known (cos θ, sin θ) — no atan2 at all (3x faster: 34 vs 100 ms per 1M evals); per-frame time oscillations computed once and shared by all cursors; neighbor scan uses squared distances, sqrt only for the close few (2x faster: 65 vs 121 ms per 2M scans).
  • Prediction (world/Predictor.js): a cosine pre-filter was benchmarked and rejected — Math.cos costs about as much as Math.acos, and survivors pay for both (48 vs 38 ms per 500k candidates: slower). Plain acos kept.
  • Flee (behaviors/Flee.js): crowd separation tested on squared distances (~2x, same family as above); (1−d/m)² written out — Math.pow(x,2) measured identical (45 vs 43 ms per 2M), kept as k*k for readability, not speed.

Deliberately not optimized: Fitts's law (Math.log2 once per gesture) — nanoseconds on a 16 ms frame budget, and the formula stays readable.
